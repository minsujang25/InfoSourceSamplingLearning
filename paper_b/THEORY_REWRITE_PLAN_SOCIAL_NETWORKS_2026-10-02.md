# Social Networks Theory Rewrite Plan
## Post-mechanism gate — October 2, 2026

The theory rewrite should now treat Experiment IV as a rank-accessibility
mechanism paper rather than a generic homophily/closure result.

## Core architecture

\[
A
\rightarrow
R_t
\rightarrow
\Pi_t
\rightarrow
\Lambda_t
\rightarrow
W_t
\rightarrow
\text{belief dynamics}.
\]

The key new bridge is the mapping from opportunity to rank depth.

### 1. Opportunity homophily

Structural opportunity determines which potentially congenial peers can
compete with the corrective source for limited attention.

Use the distinction between opportunity/induced homophily and behavioral
selection as an analogy to the classic homophily literature, while keeping the
paper's labels precise:

- structural/opportunity homophily \(H^A\);
- acquisition homophily \(H^\Lambda\);
- evidence homophily \(H^W\).

Do not equate \(H^A\) mechanically with induced homophily in the empirical
homophily literature.

### 2. Congenial crowding-out

Under segregated priors and distance-based credibility evaluation, same-group
peers are substantially more likely than other-group peers to outrank the
corrective Expert.

With a finite attention budget and recursive rank acquisition, each additional
higher-ranked peer can push the Expert into a much lower-accessibility
position.

This is the primitive Experiment-1 mechanism.

### 3. Lower-rank accessibility

The adjacent-rank probability ratio is governed by epsilon.

The epsilon robustness shows that the substantive cost of Expert demotion is
not simply ordinal rank but whether lower-ranked sources remain reachable.

The theory should therefore distinguish:

- rank depth;
- lower-rank accessibility;
- realized/effective evidence contribution.

### 4. Adaptive re-ranking

Adaptive reliance allows early credibility audits to revise source ordering.

The canonical path shows that corrective-source recovery is early-loaded but
not a one-audit artifact.

Avoid language suggesting continuous high-frequency reconfiguration over all
400 periods.

### 5. Evidence-network enclosure

Closed dependence should be defined carefully to avoid confusion with triadic
closure.

Use language such as:

- closed dependence;
- same-group dependence enclosure;
- closed evidence configuration.

Dominant cycles and Expert reach are downstream summaries of the network
configuration generated by rank accessibility. They are not causal mediation
statistics.

### 6. Corrective-route multiplicity

Experiment III should be renamed Experiment 2 and presented as a structural
network result.

Replace "redundancy" where possible with "corrective-route multiplicity" or
"gateway multiplicity."

The central claim is:

> structural corrective-route multiplicity redirects opportunity toward
> corrective gateways, and acquisition/evidence weighting largely realizes
> that structural advantage.

### 7. Social Networks literature anchors

Required positioning:

- Friedkin & Johnsen: distinguish observed relations from consequential
  influence weights; clarify that W is Bayesian evidence-precision
  contribution, not a linear influence matrix.
- Golub & Jackson (2012): connect homophily to slower learning/convergence.
- DeMarzo, Vayanos & Zwiebel (2003): repeated-information/persuasion-bias
  motivation for source-level peer evidence and tau_social.
- Kossinets & Watts (2009) and related homophily work: opportunity versus
  choice-based homophily distinction.
- stubborn-agent literature: position the fixed-biased sender as an external
  persistent influence source.
- multilayer/network-layer literature only as an analogy: A, Lambda and W are
  analytically distinct relational layers on the same nodes, not necessarily a
  conventional multiplex dataset.

### 8. Main theoretical propositions

**P1 — Opportunity is not consequential dependence.**
Structural access, acquisition, and evidence contribution must be measured
separately.

**P2 — Congenial crowding-out.**
When prior beliefs are segregated, homophilous opportunity can push corrective
sources deeper into a limited-attention ranking.

**P3 — Accessibility moderates rank displacement.**
The cost of corrective-source demotion increases as lower-ranked acquisition
becomes steeper and as the number of congenial alternatives grows.

**P4 — Re-ranking partially restores corrective access.**
Credibility reassessment can move corrective sources upward, especially early
in the learning process.

**P5 — Concentration has no invariant epistemic sign.**
Experiment 2 shows that structural concentration on corrective gateways can
improve learning.

### 9. Specification requirements

The model section must state explicitly:

- the delta/displacement Bayesian update;
- source score \(|\mu_{\delta,s}|\) under delta comparison;
- audit probability \(1/(t+1)\);
- equal acquisition during audit periods;
- no theta/state update during audit periods;
- initial delta priors;
- pre-disruption frozen initialization;
- null-last rule;
- recursive rank-acquisition probabilities.

The manuscript must be self-contained and should not refer to a preceding
individual-learning paper for definitions.

### 10. Naming

For the next draft:

- Experiment IV -> Experiment 1: Homophilous opportunity and prior segregation;
- Experiment III -> Experiment 2: Corrective-route multiplicity;
- "closure" alone -> closed dependence / evidence enclosure;
- "redundancy" -> corrective-route multiplicity where feasible.

After this theory rewrite, the next optional robustness priority is the
elite-source evidence-semantics test. Empirical calibration and clustered
topology remain optional rather than submission blockers.
